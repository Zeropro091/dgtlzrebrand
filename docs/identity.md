# Visual Identity: The Tension of Extremes

## Overview
The DGT visual identity is a bi-chromatic fusion of high-fashion editorial aesthetics and technical brutalism. It rejects the warmth of traditional "luxury" in favor of an experimental, aggressive, and highly intellectual posture. It is deliberately uncomfortable and unforgettable.

## The Color Palette
Our palette is restricted to enforce discipline. Color is not used for decoration; it is used to define space and indicate state.

*   **Electric Blue (Primary) - `#0000F2`**
    *   *Meaning*: High-voltage energy, digital raw material, the blueprint.
    *   *Application*: Large flood fills, structural borders, primary interactive elements, and monotone image overlays.
*   **Pure White (Secondary / Contrast) - `#ffffff`**
    *   *Meaning*: The void, absolute clarity, negative space.
    *   *Application*: Typography on blue, primary canvas color, high-contrast borders.
*   **Off-White (Substrate) - `#F5F5F5`**
    *   *Meaning*: The concrete foundation, raw material.
    *   *Application*: Container backgrounds or subtle section breaks to provide structural rhythm without introducing new colors.

## Typographic Tension
Our typography strategy relies on the violent collision of two opposing eras:

*   **The Classical (EB Garamond)**
    *   Represents human history, editorial authority, and permanence.
    *   Used for monolithic headlines, mission statements, and expressive moments. Tightly tracked, often pushed to extreme scales (`120px+`) to dominate the viewport.
*   **The Machine (JetBrains Mono)**
    *   Represents code, raw data, automation, and the digital grid.
    *   Used for functional, utilitarian data. Metadata, labels, navigation, and body text. Always set in uppercase for structural labels (`label-caps`).

## Photographic Degradation (Dithering)
We do not use high-fidelity, full-color photography. Reality is too soft. All imagery must be processed through a 1-bit or halftone dither filter using our Electric Blue palette. This technique strips away the organic nuance of the photograph, reducing it to mathematical pixels—a metaphor for our digital translation of physical space.

## The Visual Sensation (What You See & Experience)
*   **The Retina Burn (Color as Force)**: The Electric Blue (`#0000F2`) is not a calming, corporate blue. It is an aggressive, synthetic, high-frequency wavelength. It mimics the starkness of an uncalibrated monitor, a blueprint, or a system failure screen (BSOD). When paired against pure white, it vibrates slightly at the edges of your vision. It is visually loud.
*   **The Surgical Void (Negative Space)**: The negative space isn't empty; it feels pressurized and intentional. The stark white acts as a blinding, clinical spotlight, while the Off-White (`#F5F5F5`) feels like poured concrete or unbleached archival paper. The emptiness forces your eyes directly onto the structural elements.
*   **The Typographic Collision**: Visually, you witness two opposing eras trapped in the same room. The sweeping, organic, chiseled curves of EB Garamond (representing human history, art, and the classical) are caged inside the rigid, mathematical, monospaced grids of JetBrains Mono (representing the machine, the present, and automation).
*   **The Texture of Static (The Grain)**: When looking at the dithered photography, it is visually abrasive. You see the individual dots—the raw mathematical data of the image. It looks like crushed sand, a photocopy of a photocopy, or static electricity on a paused VHS tape. It is devoid of warmth or natural sunlight, appearing entirely synthetic and machine-rendered.
